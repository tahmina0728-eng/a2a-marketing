# Sunrise Brand Guidelines

**Enterprise Brand & Campaign System**
*Version 1.0 — Built for marketing teams, agencies, and AI-driven campaign generation systems.*

> **Brand context.** Sunrise is one of Switzerland's leading telecommunications providers, combining high-performance networks with customer-centric digital services — mobile, broadband, landline, TV, and a full suite of business connectivity, security, and ICT solutions. We connect people, businesses, and possibilities.

> **Document purpose.** A single source of truth so any team — internal, agency, or automated — can produce on-brand work without additional guidance.

## 1. Brand Overview

### 1.1 Core Promise
**Connecting people, businesses, and possibilities.**

The test every campaign, screen, and piece of copy must pass: does this help someone stay connected to what matters — simply, reliably, and with a sense of optimism?

### 1.2 Brand Philosophy
Sunrise believes technology should make life simpler, more connected, and more productive. Through premium networks and digital solutions, we help customers embrace the opportunities of an increasingly connected world. The name and the mark say it plainly: every connection is a new day breaking.

### 1.3 Mission & Vision
- **Mission:** To deliver reliable, high-performance connectivity and digital solutions that empower people and businesses across Switzerland to thrive.
- **Vision:** A Switzerland where everyone — every household, every business — is future-ready, connected without compromise.

### 1.4 Values
- **Connected** — seamless communication across every device and channel.
- **Innovative** — advanced network and digital solutions.
- **Reliable** — premium service quality and coverage.
- **Customer-focused** — solutions designed around real user needs.
- **Future-ready** — enabling digital transformation and growth.

### 1.5 Operational Promise
Three commitments under the core promise:
1. **Reliable connectivity** — mobile and broadband that simply work, everywhere we serve.
2. **High-quality digital experiences** — TV, entertainment, and services that feel effortless.
3. **Future-ready solutions** — communications built for what's next, for people and businesses alike.

### 1.6 Positioning
*Sunrise is a premium Swiss telecommunications brand delivering reliable connectivity and digital solutions that empower people and businesses to thrive in a connected future.*

- **Market role:** the leading challenger — a strong number-two in the Swiss market that competes on network quality, breadth of solutions, and customer experience.
- **Competitive edge:** extensive fixed and mobile coverage across Switzerland; end-to-end connectivity, security, and ICT from a single provider; a strong focus on innovation, diversity, and customer experience.

## 2. Brand Personality & Voice

### 2.1 Brand Archetype
**Everyman** with a **Caregiver** influence. Sunrise is accessible, human, and dependable — technology made simple and warm, never cold or elitist. We serve, we reassure, we make the complex feel easy.

### 2.2 Personality Attributes
- **Optimistic**
- **Human**
- **Clear**
- **Confident**
- **Dependable**

### 2.3 Tone of Voice
Consistent across consumer and business, app and campaign:
- **Optimistic** — every connection is a new possibility.
- **Human-centred** — people first, technology second.
- **Simple and clear** — plain language, no jargon.
- **Confident and dependable** — assured, without overpromising.

### 2.4 Voice Principle
**Say it like you'd explain it to someone you like.**

Not a call centre, not a spec sheet, not a hype machine. A knowledgeable, warm person making the connected world feel simple. Every line should pass the "would a real, kind expert say this?" test.

### 2.5 Creative Principles

| Principle | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Human before tech** | Lead with the person and the moment connectivity enables — the video call, the stream, the business that stays online. Technology is the enabler, never the hero. |
| **Simple is premium** | Clarity is our luxury. Clean layouts, plain words, generous space. We make the complicated feel effortless — that's the premium signal. |
| **Every connection is a sunrise** | Optimism is the through-line. Warmth, light, a new-day feeling. We sell possibility, not fear-of-missing-out. |

### 2.6 Connection Truths

We organise creative around small, real moments that connectivity makes possible. A great Connection Truth is:
- **Human** — a real moment in a real life or business, not an abstraction.
- **Enabled** — Sunrise is what quietly makes it possible.
- **Optimistic** — it points forward, to possibility.

Examples:
- "The call that felt like being in the room."
- "The shop that never went offline on the busiest day of the year."
- "Movie night that actually started on time."
- "The home office that works as hard as the office did."

### 2.7 Do's and Don'ts of Communication

**DO**
- Lead with the human moment, then the technology behind it.
- Use plain, warm Swiss-market-appropriate language.
- Be specific and honest about what's included.
- Keep it simple — one idea per line.
- Sound optimistic and dependable at once.

**DON'T**
- Bury the message in jargon (throughput, latency, "synergies").
- Use fear or FOMO to sell.
- Overpromise on speed, coverage, or reliability (see Section 10 — claims must be substantiated).
- Sound like a contract or a call-centre script.
- Treat business audiences as less human than consumers.

### 2.8 Voice Flexing — Consumer vs Business

The voice principle holds across both, but the register shifts:

| | Consumer | Business |
|---|---|---|
| **Focus** | The moment, the household, the feeling | The outcome, the continuity, the growth |
| **Language** | Warm, everyday, benefit-first | Warm but precise, outcome-first, credible |
| **Proof** | "Works everywhere you live" | "99.9% uptime, backed by Swiss support" |
| **Example** | "Stay connected to what matters." | "Connectivity your business can build on." |

Business is never colder than consumer — it's more precise, equally human.

### 2.9 Example Messaging
- "Stay connected to what matters."
- "Powering your digital future."
- "Connectivity without compromise."
- "The network that keeps up with your day."
- "Built for what's next."
- **Signature line:** "Connecting people, businesses, and possibilities." — used as the brand sign-off, sparingly.

## 3. Visual Identity System

### 3.1 Logo — The Sunrise Mark

The Sunrise identity is the **rising-sun mark** — a red circular ring containing a horizon line, with the sun's disc breaking across it — set above (or beside) the **Sunrise wordmark** in red.

#### Official Master Artwork
The approved vector logo files are held in the **`Logos/`** folder (see the asset map in §0.1):

| File | Colourway | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `Logos/sunrise_logo_red.svg` | Sunrise Red **#DA291C** | Light / white / off-white grounds |
| `Logos/sunrise_logo_white.svg` | White **#FFFFFF** | Sunrise Red, dark, gradient, or photographic grounds |

Both are the **primary vertical lockup** (Sun Mark above the wordmark) at a 635 × 571 viewBox, scalable to any size. **Always place the supplied vector file** — never recreate, re-trace, or screenshot the logo. These two files are the only approved colourways; see the colourway decision rule below and in the banner guidelines (§2).

#### Anatomy
- **The Sun Mark** — the ring, the horizon line, and the filled lower disc that reads as a sun rising over a horizon. This is the brand's core symbol and its central metaphor. In the master artwork the ring is an open circle broken by a horizontal horizon line, with the lower half rendered as a solid rising disc.
- **The Wordmark** — "Sunrise" set in a friendly, rounded sans, with a distinctively bold "S."

#### Construction Rules (non-negotiable)
1. **The Sun Mark always reads as a sunrise** — ring, horizon line, and rising disc intact and correctly proportioned. Never remove the horizon line or the disc.
2. **Always in Sunrise Red** on light grounds; **White** on Sunrise Red or dark grounds. Never recoloured to an arbitrary hue.
3. **Wordmark proportion and the bold "S" are fixed.** Never re-set "Sunrise" in another font.

#### Lockups
- **Vertical lockup** — Sun Mark above the wordmark. The primary lockup. Used for app icons, social profiles, square and portrait formats, hero brand moments.
- **Horizontal lockup** — Sun Mark beside the wordmark. Used for wide formats — website headers, email, signage, banners.
- **Sun Mark alone** — used as a standalone brand symbol (favicon, app icon, watermark, loading state) where the wordmark would be too small or redundant.

#### Naming
Refer to the symbol as **"the Sun Mark"** and the type as **"the Sunrise wordmark."** The full lockup is **"the Sunrise logo."**

#### Clear Space
- Clearspace equals **the diameter of the disc** within the Sun Mark, applied on all sides of the full lockup.
- Minimum size: Sun Mark **Print 8 mm** / **Digital 32 px** diameter. Full vertical lockup **Print 16 mm** / **Digital 96 px** wide.

#### Trademark
Carry ® on packaging, advertising, and corporate communications per Sunrise legal standards. Not required on internal-only materials.

#### Approval
Treated or non-standard uses (motion adaptations, co-brand lockups, off-standard placements) require Sunrise Global Brand approval at concept and pre-final stages.

#### Permitted Techniques
- **Scale** within minimum-size rules.
- **The two approved colourways** (Sunrise Red on light; White on Red/dark).
- **Motion** — the Sun Mark can rise (disc animating up across the horizon) as a signature brand animation. The wordmark stays static and legible.
- **The Sun Mark as a framing/graphic device** — the ring or the rising-disc arc can crop or frame content (see 3.2).

#### DO
- Use the Sun Mark as the primary brand anchor.
- Keep the sunrise metaphor legible — ring, horizon, disc.
- Use the vertical lockup as the default; horizontal for wide formats.
- Give the logo generous clear space.

#### DO NOT
- Remove or alter the horizon line or the rising disc.
- Recolour the mark to any colour outside Sunrise Red / White.
- Re-set the wordmark in another typeface (including Figtree).
- Rotate the Sun Mark so the sun no longer "rises" (the horizon stays horizontal, disc at the bottom).
- Add gradients, shadows, bevels, or glows to the logo.
- Stretch, skew, or distort any part.
- Place on busy backgrounds that compromise legibility.
- Use the Sun Mark as a letter substitute in words.

### 3.2 The Rising-Sun Graphic System

The Sun Mark's geometry extends into a **graphic system** for layouts and motion:
- **The Arc** — the rising-disc curve used as a horizon line across layouts, cropping images, or dividing sections.
- **The Ring** — used as a framing device around content, portraits, or key stats.
- **The Rise** — a signature motion where the disc rises across a horizon, used for reveals, loading states, and transitions.

When used as a graphic system:
- Always in Sunrise Red, the Sunrise Gradient, or White (on dark).
- Always preserving the "rising" orientation — light rises, it doesn't set.
- The full logo must still appear somewhere on the asset (the graphic system supports, it doesn't replace).

### 3.3 Signature Line — "Connecting people, businesses, and possibilities."

The brand's verbal sign-off.

- **Use with discipline.** A sign-off for hero and corporate moments, not every asset.
- **Recommended use:** brand film end cards, corporate communications, hero campaign moments, annual/investor contexts.
- **Set in:** Figtree Light (300), sentence case.
- **Colour:** Sunrise Red or Black on light grounds; White on Red/dark grounds.
- **Clearspace:** equal to the cap-height of the typeset line.
- **Localisation:** produced per the two defined locales (§5.6) — `de-CH` (default) and `en` (Global); `fr-CH`/`it-CH` follow the de-CH framework. Confirm with local brand and legal.

### 3.4 Colour Palette

Sunrise's palette is **warm, optimistic, and confident** — red as the brand signature, black and grays for structure, the sunrise gradient for energy.

#### Primary Palette

| Colour | Role | RGB | HEX |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sunrise Red** | The brand colour — the Sun Mark, primary accent, CTAs | 218.41.28 | **#DA291C** |
| **Black** | Primary type, structure, premium contrast | 0.0.0 | **#000000** |

- **Sunrise Red** is the brand. It is the Sun Mark, the primary call-to-action, the accent that says "Sunrise" at a glance. Used with confidence but not saturation — red leads, it doesn't flood.
- **Black** is structure and type. Primary headlines and body on light grounds, premium dark surfaces, and the grounding contrast that lets red and gradient sing.

#### Secondary Palette (Neutrals)

| Colour | RGB | HEX | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Charcoal** | 42.42.42 | **#2A2A2A** | Body type, dark UI surfaces, secondary structure |
| **Warm Gray** | 95.89.85 | **#5F5955** | Captions, secondary type, subtle dividers, supporting detail |

#### Tertiary Palette (Accents)

| Colour | RGB | HEX | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dawn Pink** | 222.132.158 | **#DE849E** | Soft accent, warmth, human/lifestyle moments, illustration, data highlights |
| **White** | 255.255.255 | **#FFFFFF** | Primary background, breathing room, type on dark/red |

Dawn Pink is a supporting warmth accent — used to soften, to humanise, and to add a dawn-light tint to lifestyle and illustration. It never competes with Sunrise Red as the brand colour and is never used for primary CTAs.

#### Signature Gradient

| Gradient | From | To | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sunrise Gradient** | Sunrise Red #DA291C | Sunrise Orange #FF8A00 | Hero backgrounds, the rising-sun motion, brand moments, energy surfaces. |

The Sunrise Gradient is the brand's signature energy surface — literally the colour of a sunrise, red warming into orange. Used for hero backgrounds, the Sun Mark's motion, and brand-moment fields. Direction: bottom-to-top (red low, orange high — a rising warmth) or radial from a "sun" point. Never introduce a third colour; never invert into a cold direction.

#### Functional Palette

| Colour | HEX | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Off-White** | #F9F9F9 | Soft background alternative to pure white |
| **Line Gray** | #E5E3E1 | UI dividers, card borders, table rules |

#### Colour Proportion Rules
- **White (or Off-White) dominant** as background — clean, optimistic, breathing room.
- **Sunrise Red as the confident accent** — the mark, CTAs, key highlights. Red leads but doesn't flood.
- **Black and neutrals for type and structure.**
- **Sunrise Gradient for hero and brand moments** — energy where it earns attention, not everywhere.
- **Dawn Pink sparingly** — warmth and humanity, never as a primary brand signal.

#### Colour DO / DO NOT
- **DO** build clean, white-dominant layouts with confident red accents.
- **DO** use the Sunrise Gradient for hero and brand-moment energy.
- **DO** use Black and neutrals for clear, structured type.
- **DO** use Dawn Pink to warm human/lifestyle moments.
- **DO NOT** flood layouts with saturated red (it stops being premium).
- **DO NOT** introduce colours outside the palette.
- **DO NOT** add a third colour to the Sunrise Gradient or invert it cold.
- **DO NOT** use Dawn Pink for CTAs or as the primary brand colour.
- **DO NOT** place Sunrise Red type on the Sunrise Gradient (insufficient contrast).

#### Accessibility
- WCAG 2.1 AA minimum for all body copy: 4.5:1 contrast. Telecom serves everyone — accessibility is a brand value, not a checkbox.
- **Black on White** — passes AAA. Primary body pairing.
- **White on Sunrise Red** — passes AA at all sizes. Primary pairing for red surfaces and CTAs.
- **Charcoal on White** — passes AAA. Body pairing.
- **Warm Gray on White** — passes AA for body; use for secondary text.
- **White on Black** — passes AAA.
- **Sunrise Red on White** — passes AA for large text (18 px+); use for headlines and accents, borderline for body — prefer Black for body.
- **Dawn Pink on White** — **fails contrast.** Never use as type on white; decorative/background only.
- **White on Sunrise Gradient** — passes AA on the red end; verify on the orange end (may fail at small sizes) — use large text only, or place type on a solid.
- Never rely on colour alone to convey information (e.g., service status). Always pair with label or icon.

### 3.5 Typography

Sunrise uses **one typeface: Figtree.** A friendly, modern, highly legible humanist sans — optimistic and clear, exactly on-brand. Its variable weight axis carries the full hierarchy.

#### Official Font Files
The approved font files are held in the **`Font/`** folder (see the asset map in §0.1):

| File | Type | Axis / weights |
|---|---|---|
| `Font/Figtree-Variable.ttf` | Variable, upright | Weight (`wght`) **300 → 900** (Light through Black) |
| `Font/Figtree-Italic-Variable.ttf` | Variable, italic | Weight (`wght`) **300 → 900** |

Both are **variable fonts** with a single weight axis running from **300 (Light — the signature heading weight)** to **900 (Black)**, giving every weight the brand needs — Light 300, Regular 400, Medium 500, SemiBold 600 — from one file. In production, either use a variable-font `@font-face` with `font-weight: 300 900;` and set the exact weight in CSS, or subset named instances. Always embed the supplied files; never substitute a lookalike or a differently-hinted copy.

#### Weights & Roles
Per the brand spec, **headings are set in Figtree Light (300)** — a distinctive, confident, airy display voice. Body and UI use heavier weights for legibility at small sizes.

- **Figtree Light (300)** — headlines and hero display. The signature heading weight. Large sizes only.
- **Figtree Regular (400)** — body copy, long-form, captions.
- **Figtree Medium (500)** — subheads, emphasis within body, UI labels.
- **Figtree SemiBold (600)** — CTAs, key figures, strong emphasis (used sparingly).
- **Figtree Italic** — quotes, sensory or human emphasis (available via the italic file).

#### Why Light for headings
Figtree Light at large sizes reads as open, optimistic, and premium — it lets headlines feel calm and confident rather than shouty. This is the brand's typographic signature. **The Light heading weight only works at scale** — never use 300 for body or small UI, where it loses legibility.

#### Type Roles

| Element | Font | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| Hero headline | Figtree | Light (300) |
| Section header | Figtree | Light (300) or Medium (500) at smaller scale |
| Subhead | Figtree | Medium (500) |
| Body / caption | Figtree | Regular (400) |
| Emphasis in body | Figtree | Medium (500) |
| CTA / button | Figtree | SemiBold (600) |
| Key figure / stat | Figtree | Light (300) at large scale |
| Legal / fine print | Figtree | Regular (400) at 7–8 pt |
| UI label | Figtree | Medium (500), tracked +25 |

#### Typesetting Rules
- **Headlines:** max 8 words. Sentence case. Figtree Light. Generous leading (1.1–1.2×) — Light needs air.
- **Subheads:** Figtree Medium, 1.2× leading.
- **Body:** Figtree Regular, min 16 px digital / 9 pt print, 1.5× leading.
- **Emphasis:** Figtree Medium or Sunrise Red colour — never fake-bold, never underline for emphasis.
- **Never set body or small UI in Light (300)** — legibility fails below display sizes.
- **Numerals:** tabular figures in specs, pricing, and data tables; lining figures elsewhere. Prices and speeds are proof points — set them clearly.

#### Print Type/Colour Pairings (DO)

| Surface | Type colour |
|---|---|
| White / Off-White | Black, Charcoal, or Sunrise Red (headlines) |
| Sunrise Red | White |
| Black / Charcoal | White or Off-White |
| Sunrise Gradient | White (large text only) or Black |
| Light photography | Black or Charcoal |
| Dark photography | White |

#### Digital Type/Colour Pairings (DO)

| Surface | Type colour |
|---|---|
| White | Black (body), Sunrise Red (headline/accent) |
| Sunrise Red | White |
| Black / Charcoal | White |
| Photography (light) | Black or Charcoal |
| Photography (dark) | White |

#### Forbidden in digital body copy
- Figtree Light (300) for body or small UI (legibility).
- Sunrise Red type on the Sunrise Gradient (contrast).
- Dawn Pink type on white (contrast).
- All-caps body copy (reserved for short UI labels).
- Fake-bolding via CSS — use the correct Figtree weight.

#### Licensing
Figtree is an Open Font License (OFL) typeface (Google Fonts) — free for commercial use across all Sunrise applications including digital, print, broadcast, app, and packaging, with no additional licence required and embedding permitted. The approved files are the two variable fonts in `Font/` (`Font/Figtree-Variable.ttf`, `Font/Figtree-Italic-Variable.ttf`). Always embed these supplied files in production; never substitute a lookalike or a re-hinted copy.

### 3.6 Imagery & Photography

Bright, optimistic, human, modern. Technology shown through the people and moments it enables — never as cold hardware.

#### Human Connectivity Principles
- **People and moments first.** Lead with the human — the family streaming, the founder on a call, the friends sharing a screen. The technology is felt, not featured.
- **Warm, natural, sunrise light.** Golden, optimistic, dawn-adjacent lighting. Bright and hopeful, never clinical or moody.
- **Real Swiss life.** Homes, workplaces, cities, and landscapes that reflect Switzerland's diversity — urban and rural, every region, every kind of household and business.
- **Inclusive casting.** Ages, ethnicities, family structures, abilities, business types. Everyone is connected.
- **Devices in context, never hero.** A phone in a hand mid-moment, a laptop on a real desk — shown naturally, never as a product-shot centrepiece unless the asset is specifically a device offer.

#### Business Imagery Principles
- **Outcomes, not server rooms.** Show the business succeeding — the shop open, the team collaborating, the enterprise running — not abstract data centres (unless the story is specifically infrastructure).
- **Human enterprise.** Even B2B imagery leads with people. Business is people too.
- **Credible and warm.** Professional without being sterile.

#### Composition & Emotion Rules
- Bright, clean, generous space. Optimism is a lighting and composition choice.
- The Rising-Sun graphic system (Arc, Ring) can frame or crop imagery.
- Dawn Pink and the Sunrise Gradient can tint or accent brand-moment imagery.
- Avoid: cold blue "tech" clichés, abstract network/node graphics as hero, stock-photo fakeness, fear-based or problem-first framing.

### 3.7 Iconography & Graphics
- **Rounded, friendly, geometric** — same DNA as the Sun Mark and Figtree.
- 2 px stroke at 24 px; scale proportionally.
- Outline icons for UI; filled icons for service/category markers.
- Permitted motifs: the rising arc, the ring, signal/connection lines (warm, not cold-tech), simple device glyphs.
- Sunrise Red or Black as primary; Dawn Pink as accent. Always supporting the mark and imagery.

### 3.8 Motion & Sonic
- Motion is warm and smooth — an easing that feels like a sunrise, never mechanical.
- The signature motion is **the Rise**: the sun's disc rising across the horizon within the mark, used for reveals, loading, and brand stings.
- Any sonic identity should evoke warmth and optimism — a rising, resolving motif. Approved centrally.

## 4. Product & Service Presentation

### 4.1 Offer Presentation Principles
- **Lead with the benefit, then the detail.** "Whole-home Wi-Fi that reaches every room" before the technical spec.
- **Prices and speeds are proof points** — set them clearly (Figtree, tabular figures), never bury or obscure them, and always with the legally required qualifiers (Section 10).
- **Bundles shown simply** — clear what's included, clear what it costs, clear how to get it.

### 4.2 Consumer vs Business Presentation
- **Consumer:** benefit-led, moment-led, simple. The feeling of being connected at home and on the move.
- **Business:** outcome-led, credibility-led, still human. Continuity, security, growth — proven with clear figures and Swiss support.

### 4.3 Consistency
- The Sun Mark, Sunrise Red, and Figtree appear consistently across every touchpoint — retail, app, web, care, and campaign.
- Consumer and business share one visual system; the register of imagery and copy flexes, the identity does not.

## 5. Messaging & Copywriting Framework

### 5.1 Messaging Pillars
1. **Reliable connection** — it simply works, everywhere we serve.
2. **Human experience** — technology made simple and warm.
3. **Future-ready** — built for what's next, for people and businesses.
4. **Swiss quality & breadth** — premium network, end-to-end solutions, one provider.

### 5.2 Language Patterns
- **Short, clear sentences.** Headlines under 8 words.
- **Plain language.** Avoid jargon; if a technical term is essential, explain it simply.
- **Benefit-first, proof-second.** Say what it does for the person, then back it with the fact.
- **Active, optimistic, present tense.** "Stream every room. No buffering." not "Buffering will be reduced."
- **Honest numbers.** Speeds, coverage, and prices always carry the required qualifiers and are always substantiated (Section 10).

### 5.3 Emotional Triggers
- **Optimism** (a new day, a new possibility)
- **Reassurance** (it works; you're covered)
- **Belonging** (connected to the people who matter)
- **Confidence** (ready for whatever's next)
- **Ease** (the complicated made simple)

### 5.4 Signature-Line Discipline
- "Connecting people, businesses, and possibilities." closes hero and corporate moments — not every asset.
- Set in Figtree Light.
- Localised per the two defined locales (§5.6): `en` "Connecting people, businesses, and possibilities." / `de-CH` "Wir verbinden Menschen, Unternehmen und Möglichkeiten." (`fr-CH`/`it-CH` follow the same framework). Brand- and legal-approved.

### 5.5 Localisation (Swiss market)
- **Multilingual by default.** The Swiss market spans German, French, Italian, and English. Copy must work across the market's languages, with native-speaker adaptation — never machine translation for public-facing work.
- **Translate meaning, not words.** Keep it simple and warm in every language.
- **Region-appropriate imagery and references** across Switzerland's linguistic and cultural regions.

### 5.6 Locale & Language System

Sunrise communications are authored in **two defined locales**. Every public-facing asset must be produced in the correct locale for its audience, and the choice is explicit — never assumed.

| | **Locale — Switzerland** | **Locale — Global** |
|---|---|---|
| **Language tag** | `de-CH` (Swiss High German) | `en` (International English, en-GB spelling) |
| **Role** | **Primary / default** — the Swiss home market (consumer & business) | International, corporate, investor, roaming, travel-retail, and non-DACH audiences |
| **Spelling** | Swiss High German — always **`ss`**, never `ß` (e.g. *Grüsse*, *Strasse*) | British-based international English (*colour*, *personalise*, *-ise*) |
| **Address** | **`du`** for consumer (warm, human); **`Sie`** for business & enterprise | Neutral second-person **"you"** for all audiences |
| **Currency** | `CHF 29.90` · round amounts `CHF 59.–` | `CHF 29.90` (Swiss franc retained; add conversions only where required) |
| **Thousands / decimal** | apostrophe / point → **`1'299.00`** | comma / point → **`1,299.00`** |
| **Date** | `TT.MM.JJJJ` → **`15.07.2026`** | **`15 July 2026`** |
| **Time** | 24-hour, **`14:30`** | 24-hour, **`14:30`** |
| **Quotation marks** | guillemets **`«…»`** (as used in live campaign copy) | curly double quotes **`"…"`** |

> **de-CH is the default.** For any Swiss-market asset, de-CH leads. English (Global) is used for international and corporate reach and as the fallback when a market or channel has no localised version. The Swiss French (`fr-CH`) and Italian (`it-CH`) locales follow the same rules as de-CH and inherit its address, currency, and formatting conventions — de-CH is the reference implementation for all Swiss locales.

#### Brand strings in both locales
Authoritative copy for the recurring brand lines. Machine-readable in `Guidelines/sunrise-locales.json`.

| String | `en` (Global) | `de-CH` (Switzerland) |

## 10. Compliance & Brand Safety

### 10.1 Legal & Regulatory Approvals
- **All advertising** requires clearance by Sunrise Legal before publication, with particular attention to pricing, network claims, and comparative advertising.
- **Treated logo, co-brand lockups, or off-standard uses** require Global Brand + Legal approval at concept and pre-final.
- Swiss advertising-standards (Lauterkeitskommission) and telecom-regulatory (ComCom / OFCOM) requirements apply to all consumer communications.

### 10.2 Network, Speed & Coverage Claims
- **All speed claims** ("up to X Mbit/s," "fastest network") must be substantiated, dated, sourced, and carry the legally required qualifier ("up to," conditions apply, typical vs peak). Never state a headline speed without its qualifier.
- **Coverage claims** must reflect verifiable coverage data with date and source; never imply universal coverage where it doesn't exist.
- **Comparative claims** ("Switzerland's best/fastest network," challenger positioning vs competitors) require robust, current, independent substantiation and legal sign-off. Superiority claims are high-risk and must be defensible.
- **"Unlimited" and "flat"** offers must disclose any fair-use, throttling, or condition in clear, legible terms.

### 10.3 Pricing Transparency
- All prices shown with the applicable currency (CHF), term, and any conditions (contract length, one-off fees, promotional-period expiry).
- Promotional prices must clearly state the standard price and when the promotion ends.
- No hidden fees; total cost of ownership must be discoverable.

### 10.4 Consumer Protection & Essential-Service Obligations
- Contract terms, cancellation rights, and any minimum-term conditions must be clear and accessible.
- **Emergency-services access, accessibility provisions, and any universal-service information** must be accurate and never obscured — telecom is essential infrastructure.
- Data-protection and privacy communications comply with Swiss data-protection law (revDSG) and, where relevant, GDPR.

### 10.5 Imagery & Claims Integrity
- Imagery must not misrepresent service, device, coverage, or speed.
- Device images in offers must match the device actually offered.
- Any "results" or performance depiction (e.g., streaming quality) must reflect realistic, achievable experience.

### 10.6 Cultural & Representational Safety
- Casting and storytelling reflect the full diversity of Switzerland — languages, regions, ages, ethnicities, abilities, household and business types.
- Sunrise's stated focus on diversity is reflected authentically, never tokenistically.
- No stereotypes; inclusive, human, optimistic representation throughout.

### 10.7 Confidentiality
Internal use only across the Sunrise System: employees, agencies, and approved suppliers. Not for external distribution without Legal approval.

### 11.2 Campaign Generation Rules

Any generated campaign **must**:

1. **Map to one portfolio tier** — Always-on / Brand Moment / Business (60/25/15).
2. **Lead with the human moment** — technology enables, never heroes (unless a specific device/infra story).
3. **Anchor on one Connection Truth** — human, enabled by Sunrise, optimistic.
4. **Use the Sun Mark** correctly (rising-sun intact, approved colourway).
5. **Build white-dominant** layouts with confident Sunrise Red accents; gradient reserved for hero/brand moments.
6. **Use Figtree** — Light for headlines (≥24 px), Regular/Medium for body and UI; never Light for small text.
7. **Flex register** correctly for consumer vs business.
8. **Keep copy simple and optimistic** — headline ≤8 words, plain language, no jargon.
9. **Reserve the signature line** for hero/corporate moments.
10. **CTA verb-led, ≤3 words,** Sunrise Red.
11. **Pass the "explain it to someone you like" voice test.**
12. **NEVER output an unqualified speed, coverage, or comparative claim** — every such claim needs a substantiated, approved value + qualifier, or it is blocked and routed to legal.
13. **NEVER output a price** without term, conditions, and (for promos) standard price + end date — or block and route to legal.
14. **Meet accessibility** — contrast, alt text, never colour-only for status.
15. **Locale-correct** — every asset declares its locale (`de-CH` default, or `en` Global); copy uses that locale's strings, address (`du`/`Sie`/"you"), currency, number, date, and quote conventions (§5.6). The English endline "DREAM BIG. DO BIG." is never translated. `fr-CH`/`it-CH` follow the de-CH framework.

### 11.3 Generation Workflow (Agent Sequence)

```
1. CLASSIFY → {tier, campaign_type, audience(consumer|business), channel, language, product}
2. RETRIEVE Connection Truth aligned to audience + moment
3. SET register (consumer warm-benefit | business warm-precise-outcome)
4. DRAFT hero message (<=8 words, Figtree Light, optimistic, plain)
5. DRAFT supporting message (benefit-first, proof-second)
6. IF any speed/coverage/comparative claim OR price:
     - require approved, substantiated value + qualifier

7. SELECT visual system:
     - white-dominant, Sunrise Red accent, gradient for hero only
     - human-led imagery brief, devices in context

9. CHECK accessibility (contrast, alt text, no colour-only status)
10. SET locale (de-CH default | en global) + apply locale strings/formatting per §5.6
11. VALIDATE against 11.1 -> block on any claims/pricing/compliance risk
```
