# Boozt Brand Guidelines

**Enterprise Brand & Campaign System**
*Version 1.0 — Built for marketing teams, agencies, and AI-driven campaign generation systems.*

> **Brand context.** Boozt is a hair volume and styling brand. We make sprays, root lifts, and thickening products that deliver visible volume, lift, and bounce — instantly. The stylised spelling isn't a typo; it's the brand. We don't boost. We Boozt.

> **Document purpose.** A single source of truth so any team — internal, agency, or automated — can produce on-brand work without additional guidance.

---

## 1. Brand Overview

### 1.1 Brand Promise
**Instant boozt for your hair.**

The promise every campaign, pack, and piece of copy must pass: does this deliver — or visibly show — instant lift, fullness, and energy?

### 1.2 Mission & Vision
- **Mission:** To make volume that shows up the moment you do — fast, visible, undeniable lift for every kind of hair.
- **Vision:** To own "volume" as a category the way energy drinks own "lift" — brand-as-verb, instant recognition, instant action.

### 1.3 Values
- **Visible** — if you can't see it, we didn't earn it.
- **Fast** — volume in seconds, not weeks. Results that match the pace of getting ready.
- **Bold** — the brand, the spelling, the energy. We don't apologise for any of it.
- **Inclusive** — every hair type can get a Boozt. Fine, thick, coily, straight — volume is a universal want.
- **Playful** — we take volume seriously. We don't take ourselves seriously.

### 1.4 Brand Story (positioning narrative)
"Boost" is everywhere. Energy drinks. Apps. Subscriptions. Career advice. It's the most overused promise in marketing — and the most underdelivered. We changed the spelling because we needed the brand to do what every other "boost" couldn't: stand out, stick, and back it up at the roots. Boozt is what happens when you stop accepting flat as the default.

### 1.5 Core Brand Promise (Operational)
The promise broken into three commitments:
1. **Visible lift** — at the roots, in the mirror, in the photo, immediately.
2. **Full body** — fuller, thicker-looking hair without weight or crunch.
3. **Movement** — bounce, swing, energy. Volume that moves, not volume that holds in place.

### 1.6 Global vs Local Positioning
Boozt operates as a **globally consistent masterbrand with localised expression**. The Tag, palette, italics, and voice principles are constant; talent, language, cultural references, and trend hooks adapt to the market and the moment. See Section 9.

## 2. Brand Personality & Voice

### 2.1 Brand Archetype
**Magician** primary, **Jester** secondary. We make instant transformation happen, and we have fun while doing it. We're not the Hero who slogs through a long battle — we're the one who shows up, lifts the roots, and walks out.

### 2.2 Personality Attributes
- **Punchy**
- **Confident**
- **Playful**
- **Fast**
- **Modern**

### 2.3 Tone of Voice
Consistent across pack, social, ads, app:
- **Direct** — say it, don't dance around it.
- **Witty** — humour earned through brand-name play, not gags.
- **Energetic** — the copy moves at the speed of the brand.
- **Self-aware** — we know "Boozt" is a misspelling. That's the point.

### 2.4 Voice Principle
**Speak like you've got somewhere to be.**

Not breathless, not rushed — energetic. Every line moves. Every word earns its place. If a sentence drags, it's not Boozt. Short verbs, active voice, brand name doing work.

### 2.5 Creative Principles

| Principle | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Volume is visible** | Show, don't tell. The lift in the image is the proof. We don't promise "fuller hair" — we show fuller hair. Substantiation lives in the picture. |
| **Energy is the system** | Motion in image, italics in type, brand name doing verb-work in copy. Every brand element carries forward momentum. If it stands still, it's off-brand. |
| **Loud, not noisy** | Bold colour, confident scale, italic emphasis — disciplined. Three things shouting is loud. Ten things shouting is noise. We choose three. |

### 2.6 The Brand-As-Verb Device

Boozt is a verb. It's the brand's most distinctive linguistic asset and the engine of its voice.

**Conjugation:**
- Verb form: *to boozt* — to lift, to volumise, to energise hair.
- Imperative: *boozt it, boozt up, get a Boozt.*
- Past: *boozted* (informal: "she boozted her roots before the shoot").
- Adjective: *boozted* — describing hair with visible lift.

**Approved usage patterns:**
- "Get a Boozt." (tagline / sign-off)
- "Boozt your roots."
- "Boozt your bounce."
- "Boozted in 8 seconds."
- "Flat ➝ boozted."
- "Needs a Boozt?"

**Capitalisation rule:** the proper noun "Boozt" is **always** capitalised when referring to the brand. The verb form *boozt* / *boozted* is lowercase when used as an action. "Get a Boozt." (brand). "She boozted her hair." (action).

**Don't overdo it.** One brand-as-verb moment per asset. Two if the joke earns it. Three is cringe.

### 2.7 Hair Truths

We organise creative around small, real moments from the volume journey. A great Hair Truth is:
- **Specific** — a real moment, not a generic insight.
- **Visual** — it shows up as an image, not just a line of copy.
- **Fast** — it lives in 3 seconds or less.

Examples:
- "When your hair won't stop falling flat by lunch."
- "The 8-second flip-and-shake before you leave."
- "Roots: lifted. Confidence: also lifted."
- "When the volume holds through the whole night out."

### 2.8 Do's and Don'ts of Communication

**DO**
- Lead with the lift — the visible result is the headline.
- Use the brand name as a verb. Sparingly. Confidently.
- Keep sentences short. One idea per line.
- Use italics for emphasis in display, colour for emphasis in body.
- Talk like a friend texting before they leave the house.

**DON'T**
- Use "fluffy" volume language ("ethereal lift," "weightless cloud of volume"). Boozt is direct, not poetic.
- Use the brand name as a verb more than once or twice per piece.
- Frame flat hair as a "problem" — it's the before, not the failure.
- Use "boost" anywhere in copy. The whole point of the spelling is consistency.
- Over-italicise. Italics are display. Body is upright.

### 2.9 Example Messaging
- "Get a Boozt."
- "Roots, lifted."
- "Volume, but louder."
- "Flat ➝ full. 8 seconds."
- "The 3-second root lift."
- "Boozt your bounce."
- "Hair, but make it move."

## 3. Visual Identity System

### 3.1 Logo — The Boozt Tag

The Boozt identity is a horizontal pill ("the Tag") in a brand colour, with the BOOZT wordmark in white italic uppercase inside it. Around the Tag, **two loose hand-drawn outline strokes** orbit at the edges — the brand's signature **Energy Strokes**.

#### The Three Construction Rules (non-negotiable)
1. **Always a horizontal pill shape (stadium / lozenge)** — fully rounded ends, parallel top and bottom edges. Never an oval, never a true rectangle.
2. **Wordmark always white, always italic uppercase**, centred and optically balanced inside the Tag. The italic angle is part of the identity — never reset to upright.
3. **Energy Strokes always present** — two loose hand-drawn outline strokes orbiting the Tag's edge. They are the brand's signature flourish. The Tag without Energy Strokes is incomplete.

#### Anatomy
- **The Tag** — the solid-coloured pill container.
- **The Wordmark** — BOOZT in white italic uppercase.
- **The Energy Strokes** — two hand-drawn outline strokes that trace partial arcs around the Tag's edge. One stroke runs along the upper edge into the top-right, the other along the lower edge curving up the left. They are not symmetrical and not perfectly aligned with the Tag — that imperfection is the energy.

#### Naming
Refer to the brand mark as **"the Boozt Tag"** (the full lockup) or **"the Tag"** (in-context shorthand). The strokes are **"the Energy Strokes."** Never abbreviate to "the logo" in formal documentation.

#### Clear Space
- Clearspace equals **the height of the wordmark's "O"**, applied on all four sides outside the Tag (measured from the outermost edge of the Energy Strokes, not the Tag itself).
- Minimum size: **Print 12 mm Tag width** / **Digital 80 px Tag width**.
- Below minimum size, drop the Energy Strokes and use the Tag alone (no strokes). Below half of minimum size, do not use the lockup — substitute the standalone Boozt Bug (Section 3.2).

#### Trademark
The mark requires ™ on packaging, advertising, and corporate communications until full registration; ® once registered per market.

#### Approval
Treated or non-standard uses (off-palette Tag colours, redrawn Energy Strokes, partnership co-locks, motion adaptations) require approval from **Global Brand + Legal IP** at concept and pre-final stages.

#### Permitted Techniques
- **Scale variation** — the Tag can flex large or small within minimum-size rules.
- **Colour variation** — see Section 3.4. The colour of the Tag and Energy Strokes shifts by sub-range or campaign moment.
- **Motion** — the Tag can scale, pop in/out, or bounce on entry. The Energy Strokes can animate as if drawn in real-time (a signature motion device). The wordmark stays static and legible.
- **Repetition** — the Tag can be repeated as a pattern, ticker, or stamp element in social and OOH.
- **Cropping** — large-scale crops where the Tag exceeds the canvas (OOH, full-bleed digital). Crops must never obscure the wordmark.

#### DO
- Use the full Tag + Energy Strokes lockup on every primary brand asset.
- Use the master Midnight + Boozt Blue stroke combination wherever the brand needs to read as Boozt first.
- Use colour-coded variant Tags for sub-ranges (Section 3.4).
- Let the Energy Strokes feel hand-drawn — they're meant to look slightly imperfect.

#### DO NOT
- Make the Tag oval, rectangular, or round.
- Reset the wordmark to upright (the italic is non-negotiable).
- Redraw the Energy Strokes to be perfect or symmetrical — they lose their energy.
- Place the wordmark outside the Tag.
- Add drop shadows, gradients, or 3D treatments to any part of the lockup.
- Stretch, skew, or rotate the Tag.
- Use the wordmark as a letter substitute in other words.
- Use the Tag in colours outside the approved palette (Section 3.4).
- Place the Tag over busy photography that compromises wordmark legibility.

### 3.2 The Boozt Bug

When the full Tag is too small to carry the wordmark legibly, the **Boozt Bug** is used — a simplified mark consisting of a small Tag with a stylised "B" in italic white uppercase, no Energy Strokes. Used as:
- A favicon and app icon.
- A brand-corner anchor on dense layouts.
- A pattern element in repeated form.
- A stamp/sign-off in social motion.

The Bug still follows the construction rules — pill shape, white italic wordmark, approved colour — and must appear alongside the full Tag somewhere on the asset where space allows.

### 3.3 Energy Strokes as a Standalone Graphic System

The Energy Strokes can be used as a **standalone graphic device**, separated from the Tag, to:
- Trace movement around a hair hero shot.
- Frame a value flag or callout.
- Animate as motion lines in video (drawn in, then settling).
- Repeat as a brand-coded pattern.

When used standalone:
- Always in an approved Energy Stroke colour (see Section 3.4).
- Always hand-drawn quality — never converted to perfect vector arcs.
- Must appear alongside the Tag somewhere on the asset (the Tag retains brand authorship).
- Never used as a frame around competitor product, partner logos, or third-party content.

### 3.4 Tagline — "Get a Boozt."

The brand's signature sign-off. Three words, one period, full stop.

- **Use with discipline.** It's a sign-off, not a filler. Overuse drains it.
- **Recommended use:** TVC end cards, hero campaign films, master packaging back panels, social bumpers.
- **Set in:** Rubik 700 Italic, sentence case, with the period.
- **Colour:** White on Midnight or Boozt Blue; Midnight on White; never on a busy photo background without a colour solid behind it.
- **Clearspace:** equal to the cap-height of the typeset tagline.
- **Minimum size:** Print 4 mm / Digital 14 px.
- **Localisation:** market-approved translation only. Some markets may retain the English form. Confirm with local Legal.

#### Approved tagline variations (sparingly)
- "Get a Boozt." (default)
- "Need a Boozt?" (question hook for retargeting)
- "Boozt up." (imperative shorthand for short-form social)

Never invent new tagline forms without Global Brand sign-off.

### 3.5 Colour Palette

Boozt's colour system is **a fixed master palette plus a defined range palette.** The master palette signals "Boozt"; the range palette signals "which Boozt." Discipline lives in knowing which to use when.

- **Midnight** is the master brand surface — the colour the Tag wears when the brand needs to read as Boozt before anything else. Used on corporate, master campaigns, the brand's default identity.
- **Boozt Blue** is the energy accent — Energy Strokes, CTAs, callouts, motion bumpers, and emphasis on Midnight surfaces.

Each sub-range adopts a Tag colour and an Energy Stroke colour. The wordmark stays white. Every other rule (construction, italics, strokes-orbit-the-Tag) stays identical. This is how Boozt extends without losing itself.

Range colours appear on the Tag, packaging primary surface, in-store strips, and as campaign accent for range-led work. Range colours **never** appear on the Master Tag (which is always Midnight), never combined with another range's colour in a single composition, and never invented for a single campaign without Global Brand sign-off.

#### Functional Palette (Limited Use)

The functional palette is the **only** acceptable extension of master and range palettes. No additional brand colours, no decorative tints, no gradient variants (gradient is allowed within Master Palette in motion only, not in static design).

#### Colour Proportion Rules
- **Midnight dominant** in master-brand work; **range colour dominant** in range-led work.
- **White as primary background** for long-form digital, packaging text panels, and clean editorial.
- **Boozt Blue and Sky as accents and CTAs** — never as primary surface fills outside of motion gradients.
- Generous breathing room. Negative space is brand-active.

#### Engagement-Based Colour Volume

| Engagement Length | Description | Brand Volume | Product Volume |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Scroll** (≤3s) | TikTok hook, OOH, end cards | 10 | 1 |
| **Watch** (1–2 min) | Social, banner, video tutorial | 6 | 5 |
| **Routine** (2+ min) | Routine page, in-app, packaging back | 2 | 10 |

#### Colour DO / DO NOT
- **DO** anchor every master-brand asset with the Midnight Tag and Boozt Blue Energy Strokes.
- **DO** let a range colour lead when the campaign is range-led — the Tag and Strokes adopt that range's palette for the moment.
- **DO** use White as the primary breathing-room base for long-form content.
- **DO** use Boozt Blue or Sky for CTAs and emphasis on Midnight surfaces.
- **DO** allow Midnight ➝ Sky gradients in motion only (loading bars, video bumpers, transitions).
- **DO NOT** introduce colours outside master or range palettes.
- **DO NOT** combine two range colours in the same composition.
- **DO NOT** use range colours on the Master Tag.
- **DO NOT** apply static gradients to brand surfaces (motion only).
- **DO NOT** use Sky as a body-text colour on White — fails contrast.

#### Accessibility
- WCAG 2.1 AA minimum for all body copy: 4.5:1 contrast.
- **White on Midnight** — passes AAA. Primary type pairing for the Tag.
- **White on Boozt Blue** — passes AA at all sizes.
- **Charcoal on White** — passes AAA. Primary body-text pairing.
- **Boozt Blue on White** — passes AA at 18 px+ only. Use for headlines, never body.
- **Sky on White** — **fails contrast.** Never use Sky as type on White.
- **White on Sky** — **borderline.** Use only for large display type (24 px+).
- Never rely on Tag colour alone to identify a sub-range — always pair with the range name in type.

### 3.6 Typography

Boozt uses **one typeface, two voices**: Rubik 700 Italic carries the brand at scale; Rubik upright carries it at reading distance. The italic-vs-upright contrast is the brand's typographic signature.

#### Rubik 700 Italic — Display
The brand's hero voice. Every headline, every variant name on pack, every hero callout. The italic angle mirrors the wordmark and carries forward momentum across every asset.

- **Use only for type 24 px / 14 pt and larger.** Italic is hard to read in body sizes.
- **Always italic, always 700 weight.** Other italic weights (300, 400, 500) are not approved for display.
- Sentence case for editorial headlines; UPPERCASE for short, punch headlines (≤4 words) and sub-range names on pack.
- Tight leading (1.0–1.1×).
- Display tracking: −10 to −25 at 60 pt and above. The brand's italic feels more confident when slightly tighter than default.

#### Rubik (Variable, Upright) — Body & UI
Body, subheads, UI, microcopy, recipe steps, captions. Variable axis on weight — use Regular and Medium as primary, Light for editorial bodies, Bold only for callouts (rare).

- **Rubik Medium** — subheads, navigation, captions on hero assets, emphasis within body.
- **Rubik Regular** — body copy, routine steps, packaging instructions, captions.
- **Rubik Light** — long-form editorial body where Regular would feel heavy.
- **Rubik Bold** — rare. Used only for inline emphasis where Boozt Blue colour alone doesn't earn the moment.

**Never use upright Rubik for hero headlines.** That role is locked to 700 Italic.
**Never use Rubik Italic in body weights** (300, 400, 500) — italic in this brand is *only* the display voice.

System fallback (where the brand font cannot be embedded): Helvetica Italic → Arial Italic → sans-serif italic (for display); -apple-system → Inter → sans-serif (for body).

#### Type Roles

| Element | Font | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| Hero headline (TVC, OOH, hero social) | Rubik | 700 Italic |
| Sub-range name on pack | Rubik | 700 Italic, UPPERCASE |
| Brand callout / sticker | Rubik | 700 Italic |
| Subhead | Rubik | Medium (upright) |
| Body / routine step / caption | Rubik | Regular (upright) |
| Long-form editorial body | Rubik | Light (upright) |
| Tagline | Rubik | 700 Italic |
| UI button label | Rubik | Medium, UPPERCASE, tracked +25 |
| Legal / back-of-pack | Rubik | Regular at 7–8 pt |

#### Typesetting Rules
- **Headlines:** max 8 words. Default sentence case; UPPERCASE allowed only for ≤4 words.
- **Subheads:** sentence case, 1.2× leading, Rubik Medium upright.
- **Body:** sentence case, 1.5× leading, Rubik Regular upright. Min 16 px digital / 9 pt print.
- **Emphasis in body:** Boozt Blue colour. Don't italicise body. Don't bold body (rare exception, see above).
- **Numerals:** tabular figures in pricing, ingredient lists, and routine steps. Lining figures elsewhere.
- **The italic/upright rule is the discipline.** If you've italicised body copy, you're off-brand. If you've used upright Rubik for a hero headline, you're off-brand.

#### Print Type/Colour Pairings (DO)

| Surface | Type colour |
|---|---|
| White | Charcoal, Midnight, or Boozt Blue (headlines only) |
| Midnight | White |
| Boozt Blue | White |
| Range Tag colour | White (sub-ranges follow their own pairing rules — see Section 3.5) |
| Light photography | Charcoal or Midnight |
| Dark photography | White |

#### Digital Type/Colour Pairings (DO)

| Surface | Type colour |
|---|---|
| White | Charcoal (body), Midnight (subhead), Boozt Blue (display headline only) |
| Midnight | White |
| Boozt Blue | White |
| Photography (light) | Charcoal |
| Photography (dark) | White |

#### Forbidden in digital body copy
- Rubik 700 Italic for body copy at any size (italic is reserved for display).
- Sky as type on White (insufficient contrast).
- Boozt Blue type at body sizes under 18 px.
- All-caps body copy (reserved for short headlines and UI labels).
- Fake italics via CSS skew on non-italic Rubik (always use the licensed italic file).

#### Licensing
Rubik (Open Font License, Google Fonts) is the licensed Boozt typeface in both upright and italic variable forms — free for commercial use across all applications including digital, print, broadcast, app, and packaging. No additional licence required.

If a display or partnership font is ever proposed (limited-edition campaign, collab), Legal approval is required and the licence must explicitly cover the intended application.

### 3.7 Imagery & Photography

#### Hair Photography Principles

- **Volume is the subject.** Every hero hair shot must visibly demonstrate the lift, fullness, or body the product delivers. If the volume isn't visible in the photograph, the photograph isn't on-brand.
- **Light for the lift.** Backlight or three-quarter light to reveal hair separation, root height, and silhouette. Avoid flat overhead light that compresses volume.
- **Motion-friendly framing.** Show hair mid-toss, mid-shake, mid-walk. Stillness is the enemy of an energy brand. Even seated shots should imply motion.
- **The mirror moment.** A signature beat — the wearer checks the lift, smiles, leaves. Used in TVC end frames, social closes, and brand-moment finals.
- **Authentic lift.** Volume on screen must match volume the product delivers. No off-brand backcombing, no retouched root height. Substantiation lives in the image.
- **Show the range.** A complete brand photo set covers, at minimum: straight fine, wavy fine, curly fine, straight medium, wavy medium, curly medium, coily fine, coily medium. Every hair type can be boozted.

#### Lifestyle Photography Principles

- **Pre-going-out energy.** The brand lives in the 8 minutes between finishing hair and walking out the door. That's the emotional space.
- **Real spaces, fast moments.** A real bathroom, a real bedroom, a real friend's couch. Mid-getting-ready, never staged perfection.
- **Inclusive casting.** Range across ages (16–35 core, with 35+ permitted), ethnicities, hair types, gender expressions. No single archetype.
- **Hands and gestures.** Spraying, lifting, flipping, shaking, tousling. Action verbs in the frame.
- **Self-shot energy.** Boozt's lifestyle photography should feel close to selfie distance — like the wearer took the photo, not a crew.

#### Composition & Emotion Rules

- **Dynamic compositions.** Diagonal lines, off-centre subjects, mid-motion crops. Static centred portraits are the exception.
- **High-key or contrast-key lighting.** No moody, low-key shoots. The brand is energy, not introspection.
- **Real spontaneity.** A laugh, a flip, a sideways glance — earned moments, not posed beats.
- Avoid: studio beauty-counter gloss, slow-mo melancholy edits, monochrome desaturation that strips the brand's colour energy, "before" frames that look depressing.

#### "Before / After" Discipline

Volume is a category that lives on demonstrating change. Boozt's before/after rules:
- **Same talent, same shot, same light, same day.** No different models, no different rooms, no different lenses.
- **The "before" is flat, not failed.** Frame the before as the wearer's normal-but-flat hair, not as a problem state.
- **No despair in the before.** Never sad-faced, never lit moodily, never desaturated.
- **The "after" is the wearer's hair, lifted.** Same person, same outfit, same context — just boozted.
- **Substantiated:** before/after must be from a single shoot with talent release and time stamps on file. No composite shots.

### 3.8 Iconography & Graphics

- **Rounded, slightly italic-leaning geometry** — same DNA as the wordmark.
- 2 px stroke at 24 px size; scale proportionally.
- Outline icons for UI; filled icons for hair-type indicators and sub-range markers.
- Permitted illustrative motifs: motion lines (echoing Energy Strokes), upward arrows, sparkle/pop bursts (used sparingly), spray cans, root-lift cross-sections.
- Always supporting the photography and the Tag — never competing.

### 3.9 Sonic Identity *(optional, in development)*

If sonic is rolled out, the brand mnemonic should evoke lift — a short upward two-note motif, a spray-and-pop sound, or a fast ascending sweep. Used after the tagline as an audio sign-off. Pure form in Always-on; reinterpretations allowed in Brand Moments with approval.

## 4. Product Presentation Guidelines

### 4.1 Styling Principles
- **Hero the result first.** The hair, the lift, the volume. Pack appears as supporting evidence.
- **When pack leads** (NPD, value, retail-specific), shoot product cleanly against the range colour or White — never against busy photography.
- **Show the application moment** — the spray mid-air, the bottle tilted, the pump pressed. Action reads stronger than still life.
- **Volume cues in product shots.** Hair around the bottle should still show lift. The product isn't on a neutral table; it's in the volume moment.

### 4.2 Realism vs Aspiration
Boozt sits at **bold but honest**. The volume on screen must be achievable by a real user. We don't retouch root height, we don't paint in fullness, we don't imply effects the formula doesn't deliver. Bold ≠ false.

### 4.3 Consistency Across Markets
- The Tag, the Wordmark, the Energy Strokes, and the Master Palette appear identically on every pack worldwide.
- Pack architecture (Tag placement, range colour, hero photography, italics) follows a single global template.
- Local markets honour their own talent, languages, and trend hooks — but the system stays locked.
- Variant typography, layout, and brand voice remain globally consistent.

## 5. Messaging & Copywriting Framework

### 5.1 Messaging Pillars

Every campaign ladders up to one or more:

1. **Instant Lift** — visible volume in seconds.
2. **Brand-As-Verb** — owning "boozt" as a word.
3. **Movement & Bounce** — volume that moves with you.
4. **Energy & Confidence** — the emotional payoff of being boozted.

### 5.2 Language Patterns
- **Short sentences.** Headlines under 8 words. Body under 14 words per sentence.
- **Active verbs.** Lift, boozt, pop, lift, move, shake, flip. Avoid linking verbs.
- **Specific over generic.** "+200% root volume in 8 seconds" beats "fuller-looking hair." (Substantiation required.)
- **Brand-as-verb.** Once per piece, twice max. Earn it.
- **Numbers when honest.** "8 seconds," "3 sprays," "+200% root lift" — always substantiated with study or claim citation.

### 5.3 Emotional Triggers
- **Confidence** (the mirror beat, the leaving-the-house beat)
- **Speed** (the rush of fast results)
- **Self-expression** (volume as fashion accessory, as style choice)
- **Belonging** (the trend, the look, the moment everyone's having)
- **Play** (boozt as a fun verb, as a wink)

### 5.4 Tagline Discipline
- "Get a Boozt." is a closing line — TVC end cards, signature campaigns, master packaging.
- "Need a Boozt?" is a retargeting hook — used in returning-shopper digital only.
- "Boozt up." is short-form social shorthand — used in TikTok, Reels, Stories.
- Always set in Rubik 700 Italic with the period.
- Localised translations only when Legal-approved. Some markets may keep the English form to preserve the brand-as-verb identity.

### 5.5 Localisation Guidelines
- **Translate the verb.** Where possible, build a local-language equivalent that preserves the brand-as-verb device. Where not possible, retain the English "Boozt" and adapt surrounding copy.
- **Lead with local talent and trends.** Volume looks different on different hair types in different cultures — every market's photography is its own.
- **Local pricing and pack sizes localise; brand architecture does not.**

## 6. Audience Segmentation

Boozt is a youth-led brand. The core audience is **16–34**, social-first, trend-aware. Segmentation cuts by **relationship with volume**, not by age alone.

### 6.1 The Trend Chaser
- **Insight:** Watches volume trends on TikTok, replicates the look, posts the result.
- **Hooks:** Trend-tied content, creator collabs, fast-execution tutorials, limited-edition drops.
- **Tone:** Punchy, trend-fluent, social-native. "The Boozt trend on your FYP this week."

### 6.2 The Flat-Hair Frustrated
- **Insight:** Has fine, flat, or limp hair. Has tried everything. Wants something that actually works.
- **Hooks:** Substantiated claims, before/after content, ingredient transparency.
- **Tone:** Direct, evidence-led, no nonsense. "The volume that holds. Substantiated."

### 6.3 The Style Switcher
- **Insight:** Changes hair often. Loves the styling moment. Volume is one expression among many.
- **Hooks:** Multi-format range, style versatility, occasion-led content.
- **Tone:** Versatile, encouraging, occasion-aware. "Volume for the night out. Volume for the meeting. Volume for the post."

### 6.4 The Going-Out Get-Ready
- **Insight:** Volume is part of the pre-going-out ritual. Boozt is in the 8-minute window before leaving.
- **Hooks:** Speed content, "get ready with me" formats, occasion timing.
- **Tone:** Pre-going-out energy, friend-text speed. "8 seconds. Out the door."

### 6.5 The Routine Loyalist
- **Insight:** Has a fixed hair routine. Boozt is the volume layer in a multi-step regimen.
- **Hooks:** Routine-builder content, multi-product bundles, sub-range education.
- **Tone:** Specific, ingredient-fluent, routine-respecting. "Where Boozt sits in your stack."

## 7. Campaign System Framework

### 7.1 Campaign Portfolio Framework

| Tier | Share | Role | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Always-on / Core** | 60% | Sustaining presence across the range. Social-led, routine content, in-store, value. | TikTok tutorials, before/after Reels, app deals, range awareness. |
| **Brand Moments** | 25% | Tentpole campaigns that stretch identity or own a cultural / category moment. | Festival season volume, party season campaign, brand-as-verb hero campaign. |
| **Partnerships / Collabs** | 15% | Co-created with creators, stylists, or cultural collaborators. | TikTok creator collabs, stylist limited editions, music-tied moments. |

### 7.2 Campaign Types

#### 7.2.1 Product Launch
- Hero the lift visible in the photography.
- One Hair Truth as hero message.
- The product's range colour earns its visual moment.
- TikTok + Reels lead; TVC supports for major launches.
- Before/after framing where substantiated.

#### 7.2.2 Seasonal / Cultural Moment
- Festival, party, awards, back-to-school, NYE — moments where volume matters more.
- Often a Brand Moment with treated Tag latitude (with approval).
- Always begins with the moment, not the product.

#### 7.2.3 Value Campaign
- Bold Rubik 700 Italic typography on Midnight or White.
- Clear price, clear pack, clear benefit.
- Sub-range colour where applicable.
- Copy: ≤6 words headline. Single CTA.

#### 7.2.4 Brand Campaign
- Identity-led storytelling. Permission for energy, brand-as-verb play, manifesto copy.
- Hero is the wearer mid-getting-ready. Pack appears only as a witness.
- Tagline at sign-off — earned.

#### 7.2.5 Creator / Stylist Partnership
- Co-created within Boozt's rules. The Tag stays Midnight unless the partnership is range-led.
- Creator's voice and Boozt's voice intersect; Boozt is the host.
- Always result-led: the partnership exists for the visible lift.

### 7.3 Campaign Components

Every campaign has:

1. **Hero Message** — one Hair Truth or product truth, max 8 words, Rubik 700 Italic.
2. **Supporting Message** — 1–2 lines, Rubik Medium upright.
3. **Visual System** — hair-led hero photography + Boozt Tag (master or range) + Energy Strokes.
4. **CTA Pattern** — verb-led, ≤3 words: "Get a Boozt" / "Shop the Lift" / "See the Range" / "Try the Volume."
5. **Sign-off** — Tag + Energy Strokes + (optional) tagline.

### 7.5 Content Templates

#### 7.5.1 TikTok / Reels Hero (9–15s)
- **0–1.5s:** Hook — flat hair close-up, or wearer pre-style, or a trend-tied opener.
- **1.5–3s:** The Boozt moment — spray, lift, shake. Visible change.

## 8. Digital & UX Guidelines

### 8.1 App & Website Tone
- **Speed-first utility**, brand voice second. Get the user to the right product quickly, then add the brand-as-verb wink.
- Error states stay punchy: "That didn't work. Try again?" — not "Sorry, an error occurred."
- Confirmations short and on-brand: "Cart boozted. Ready to check out."

### 8.2 UI Consistency Principles
- White as primary surface; Midnight for brand-anchor moments and headers; Boozt Blue for primary CTAs.
- Range colours for range-specific contexts (a Repair sub-range page adopts Burgundy accents).
- Charcoal type on White for body throughout digital.
- The Tag used as a UI sticker for badges, callouts, and saved-item containers.
- Rubik italic for hero/display, Rubik upright for everything else. Italic discipline is the brand's UI fingerprint.

### 8.3 Microcopy Guidelines
- **CTAs:** verb-led, ≤3 words. "Get a Boozt," "Shop the Lift," "Match My Hair," "Build Routine."
- **Empty states:** punchy. "Nothing saved yet — let's fix that →"
- **Confirmations:** specific. "Boozt Volume Spray added. Looking lifted."
- **Loading:** brief and on-brand. "Boozting…"

### 8.4 Accessibility
- WCAG 2.1 AA minimum.
- Alt text on every hair image — descriptive ("a close-up of fine straight hair lifted at the roots") not promotional.
- Routine and ingredient lists usable by screen readers with structured numbered lists.
- Don't rely on Tag colour alone to identify range — always pair with the range name in type.
- Captions on every video, audio description where relevant. TikTok auto-captions checked and corrected, never trusted raw.

## 10. Compliance & Brand Safety

### 10.1 Legal Approvals
- **All advertising materials** require clearance by local Boozt counsel or Global Marketing/IP Legal before publication.
- **Treated Tag, redrawn Energy Strokes, off-palette colour variants,** or partnership co-locks require Global Brand + Legal IP approval at concept and pre-final stages.
- **New trademarks, slogans, sub-range names,** or brand-as-verb extensions require Legal review before any external use.

### 10.2 Fonts & Licensing
- Rubik (Open Font License via Google Fonts) is the licensed Boozt typeface, free for commercial use across all applications including digital, print, broadcast, app, and packaging.
- Any non-Rubik usage requires a verified commercial-use licence covering the application and Legal sign-off.

### 10.3 Cosmetic Claims & Imagery
- **Volume claims** ("+200% root lift," "fuller-looking hair," "8-second volume") require substantiation by clinical study or consumer-perception research per market regulation. Always verify with Regulatory before publication.
- **Before/after imagery** must be from a single shoot, same talent, same lighting, same day. Talent release and timing on file. No composites.
- **Imagery must reflect achievable results.** No retouched root height. No painted-in volume. The visible lift on screen must match the lift the product delivers.
- **INCI ingredient lists** mandatory on pack and digital product pages per local regulation.
- **Allergen and fragrance disclosure** follow local regulation (EU FIC, US FDA, etc.).
- **Sustainability claims** require documented supply-chain verification. Vague claims ("clean," "natural," "eco") without specifics are off-brand and high-risk legally.

### 10.4 Cultural & Representational Safety
- Casting must reflect the breadth of the audience — across hair types, ethnicities, ages, body types, and gender expressions.
- Stylists working on shoots must be experienced with the hair types being shot.
- Trend participation requires cultural awareness — never participate in trends Boozt hasn't earned the right to participate in, and never appropriate from communities without crediting and compensating.
- Avoid: framing flat hair as a failure state, frame-shaming, body-comparative volume messaging, beauty hierarchies.

### 10.5 Confidentiality
Internal use only across the Boozt System: employees, franchisees, agencies, suppliers, distributors, salon partners. Not for external distribution without Legal approval.

### 11.2 Campaign Generation Rules

Any generated campaign **must**:

1. **Map to one portfolio tier** — Always-on / Brand Moment / Partnership (60/25/15).
2. **Hero the visible lift, not the pack.** Pack is a witness, not the headline.
3. **Anchor on one Hair Truth** — specific, visual, fast (≤3 seconds to read).
4. **Apply the right Tag colour:**
   - Master Midnight for corporate, master campaigns, brand moments.
   - Range colour when the campaign is range-led.
5. **Include Energy Strokes** on every full Tag lockup.
6. **Use colour proportion** appropriate to engagement length:
   - Scroll (≤3s): brand 10, product 1
   - Watch (1–2 min): brand 6, product 5
   - Routine (2+ min): brand 2, product 10
7. **Use Rubik 700 Italic for display, Rubik upright for body.** Never reverse the rule.
8. **One range colour per composition.** Never mix two range colours.
9. **Brand-as-verb used max twice** per asset. Default once.
10. **Tagline reserved for sign-off.** Not used in workhorse comms.
11. **CTA verb-led, ≤3 words.**
12. **Pass the "speak like you've got somewhere to be" voice test.**
13. **No forbidden language** — never "boost," "ethereal," "miracle," "magic," "problem hair."
14. **No regulated performance claims** without Regulatory clearance.
15. **Volume must be visible** in every hero image — if the lift isn't in the picture, regenerate.
