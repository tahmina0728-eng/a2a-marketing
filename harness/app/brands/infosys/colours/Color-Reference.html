A palette built on Infosys Blue

Infosys Blue is the cornerstone; Infosys Chrome stabilises it for critical messaging. Screening (tints) of the primary colours is not permitted. Six colour sets extend the system — never mix two sets in one asset, and never use gradients.

Every colour on this page carries its measured contrast ratio, so the legal text colour for any background is stated rather than guessed. On colour — banners, activation panels, any filled field — the type is white; when a ratio falls short the ground gets darker, not the type. Three Medium tiers are signed off to carry white below AA as documented exceptions. Normal text needs 4.5:1, large text (24px regular / 18.66px bold and above) needs 3:1, and icons, borders and focus rings need 3:1.

Documented exceptions

Three grounds are approved to carry white type below the AA thresholds. They are exceptions by decision, not by oversight — the measured ratio is recorded against each so nobody re-litigates it later.

Where the exception applies

  • Campaign key visuals and social banners. Event titles, session names and campaign headlines set in white on a full Coral, Jade or Topaz Medium field.
  • Activation Portal hero panels. The Medium tier is the Activation Portal colour; white display type on it is the intended treatment.
  • Stat and call-out figures. Large numerals with a short white label, where the figure is the whole message.
  • Sub-brand campaign moments. Where the set colour is doing the recognition work and swapping it would read as a different brand.

Where it does not

  • Body copy, captions and disclaimers. At small sizes these three grounds are not legible in white — use the set’s Dark tier as the ground instead.
  • Anything interactive. Buttons, form labels, links, focus and error states keep to the AA pairings in this section.
  • Product UI and documentation. The exception is a campaign device; it does not travel into the product.

The exception is scoped to these three named colours. It is not a general licence to drop AA — every other pairing in this reference still holds, and an off-palette ground sampled from photography is not covered by it.

06 — Banner grounds · white text

White is the text colour on colour

Every campaign banner sets its type in white, and that stays. The variable is not the text colour — it is the ground behind it and the size the text is rendered at. These seven live banners are measured below against white.

Read the ratio as a size budget, not a colour switch. White needs 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text (24px regular / 18.66px bold and above). Where a ground falls short, darken the ground — never flip the type to Sapphire Dark or black. Dark type on a photographic or mid-tone banner breaks the campaign look and reads as a different brand.

08 — Rules that still stand

  • No screening of primary colours. Use the Sapphire set for lighter and darker steps.
  • Never mix two colour sets in one asset.
  • No gradients, including chart fills and button states.
  • No colours outside the brand sets — including opacity-derived greys and anything a third-party UI library introduces.
  • Dark tiers are not layout backgrounds. The exception is digital surfaces where Sapphire Dark is the reading background (17.57:1 with white).
  • Red is Finacle only.
  • Tier discipline: Ultra Light for backgrounds and illustration, Light for Portals and illustration, Medium for the Activation Portal, backgrounds and call-outs, Dark for typography, line art and logos.
  • AA is the floor, with three named exceptions. Coral, Jade and Topaz Medium carry white type below AA by brand decision — see section 05. Everywhere else, where dark type on a light brand background can reach AAA without breaking the palette, take it.
  • White is the type colour on any coloured ground. If white does not clear the ratio at the size required, change the ground — never the type.
  • Judge large text at rendered size. Social cards are scaled down in feed; a headline that qualifies at 1200 px may not at 500 px.
  • Never rely on colour alone to carry meaning.
